## What the mechanic does

The car is raised on a lift or jack stands and the wheel is removed to expose
the brake caliper. The caliper is unbolted and lifted away from the disc, then
supported on a hook or stand so its flexible hose is never left carrying the
weight. The worn pads slide out of the carrier.

Before the new pads go in, the caliper piston has to be pushed back into its
bore to make room for the extra thickness. The mechanic opens the brake fluid
reservoir first, because pushing the piston back forces fluid up the line and
an already-full reservoir will overflow.

The new pads are fitted with their anti-rattle shims and clips, the caliper is
bolted back to the specified torque, and the wheel goes back on. The brake
pedal is then pumped before the car is moved, so the piston takes up the gap
and the first press of the pedal is not an empty one.

## How long it takes

Front pads on a common car take roughly one to two hours for an axle pair.
Add time if the discs also need replacing or skimming, if the caliper slide
pins have seized, or if the wheel bolts have been over-tightened by an impact
gun at a previous service.

## Pads are always replaced in axle pairs

Both wheels on the same axle are done together, even if only one side is worn.
Different pad thicknesses left and right make the car pull to one side under
braking. A garage that offers to replace a single pad is cutting a corner that
you will feel at the first hard stop.

## The discs matter too

Pads wear against the disc, and a disc has a minimum safe thickness stamped on
it. If the disc is below that figure, or is scored, warped or lipped at the
edge, new pads on a worn disc will wear quickly and unevenly. Ask whether the
discs were measured. A garage doing the job properly will tell you the reading.

## Bedding in the new pads

New pads need a bedding-in period, usually the first 200 to 300 km. During
this time the pad material transfers a thin even layer onto the disc face.
Avoid heavy braking from high speed during this period where you safely can.
Braking may feel slightly softer than usual until bedding is complete.

## What to check afterwards

Look through the wheel spokes at the new pad thickness so you know what new
looks like on your car. Ask for the old pads back, or at least to see them.
Check that the brake fluid reservoir is between the minimum and maximum marks
and that the cap is properly seated. On the first drive, the pedal should feel
firm and the car should stop straight without pulling.
