## Ask for the old parts

The simplest check available to any driver is to ask for the removed parts
back, or at least to be shown them, before the car leaves. A worn pad, a used
filter or a dead battery is immediate evidence that a replacement happened. It
is a normal request and a reasonable garage will not object to it.

## Read the invoice against the quote

The invoice should name the part fitted, not just the job. A part number,
brand and quantity turn a line item into something checkable. Labour should be
separate from parts. If a quoted job grew during the work, there should be a
reason recorded for it, and ideally a call before it was carried out rather
than an explanation afterwards.

## Warranty is on two separate things

A part carries a manufacturer warranty, and the fitting carries a workmanship
warranty from the garage. They are different, they usually run for different
lengths of time, and a cheap part fitted well can still fail while a good part
fitted badly is the garage's responsibility. Establish both before agreeing to
the work, and keep the invoice, because a warranty claim without proof of
fitting date is difficult.

## Genuine, aftermarket and counterfeit

Genuine parts come from the vehicle manufacturer, aftermarket parts from
independent makers, and a good aftermarket brand is often the sensible choice
on cost. Counterfeit parts imitating known brands are a real problem for
friction and electrical components in particular. Buying through an
established supplier, and checking that packaging and part numbers look right,
is the practical defence.

## Questions worth asking before agreeing

Ask what the total is likely to be including labour, and what would make it go
higher. Ask whether related items should be done at the same time, since much
of the labour is shared and doing them separately costs more in the end. Ask
how long the car will be off the road. For a wear item, ask what was measured
and what the reading was.

## Signs of a garage worth returning to

Willingness to show you the measurement rather than assert the conclusion. An
itemised written quote before the work. A clear answer about which parts are
being fitted and why. Not being pushed into extra work on the day without
explanation. Rating and review count are a useful starting filter, but a
garage that explains its reasoning is the one that lets you check anything at
all.

## Keeping a service history

Recording each service with the date, the odometer reading and what was done
is what makes the next interval calculable rather than guessed. It also
supports the resale value of the vehicle, and it is what allows a maintenance
prediction to be anchored to a known starting point rather than an estimate.
