## Why these codes are filed under Battery but are usually not the battery

P0562 means system voltage is too low, P0563 means it is too high, and P0620
points at the alternator control circuit. All three are charging faults, and
in most cases the battery is the victim rather than the cause.

This matters because the obvious reaction to a battery warning is to buy a
battery. If the alternator is not charging properly, a new battery will go
flat exactly like the old one did, and the driver has paid twice.

## How the mechanic finds the cause

The test is quick and needs only a multimeter. With the engine off a healthy
battery reads about 12.6 volts. With the engine running the reading should
rise to roughly 13.8 to 14.4 volts, which shows the alternator is charging.

A reading that does not rise when the engine starts means the alternator, its
belt, or its wiring. A reading that rises far above 14.4 volts means the
voltage regulator is failing and is overcharging the battery. A reading that
looks correct at both points sends attention back to the battery itself and to
the terminals.

Terminals are checked before anything is replaced. Corrosion on a terminal
produces intermittent starting that looks exactly like a dying battery, and
cleaning it costs nothing.

## What each fault damages if ignored

Undercharging means the battery is never brought back to full after starting.
A lead acid battery left partly discharged sulphates internally and loses
capacity permanently, so a charging fault left alone destroys the battery as
well, and eventually the car will not start at all.

Overcharging is more damaging and faster. It boils off the electrolyte, can
swell or crack the case, and the raised system voltage stresses every
electronic module on the car.

## How long the repair takes

Testing the charging system takes minutes. Replacing an alternator is
typically two to three hours depending on how buried it is. Replacing a drive
belt is under an hour. A battery replacement is twenty to thirty minutes.

## What to check afterwards

Ask for the charging voltage reading with the engine running, both before and
after the work. That single number is what proves the fault is fixed. If a
battery was replaced because of one of these codes, ask specifically what was
found to be causing the charging fault, because the battery alone does not
explain a P0562.
