## What the engine light actually means

The light means the car's computer has recorded a fault it considers
significant. It does not say how serious the fault is, and it does not say
what is wrong. A loose fuel cap and a failing catalytic converter light the
same lamp.

A steady light means a fault is stored and the car can normally be driven to a
garage. A flashing light means damage is happening right now, most often a
severe misfire pushing raw fuel into the exhaust, and means stop driving.

## Confirmed, pending and permanent codes

A pending code means a test failed once. The computer waits to see whether it
fails again on a later drive before lighting the lamp, because a single odd
reading is not proof of a fault. Pending codes are useful precisely because
they appear before the light does.

A confirmed code means the test failed repeatedly and the lamp is on.

A permanent code cannot be erased by clearing. It stays until the car itself
confirms the fault is genuinely gone across several drive cycles. It exists
specifically to stop a fault being hidden by resetting the light before a test.

## Freeze frame: the conditions when it happened

When a fault is stored the computer also saves a snapshot of what the engine
was doing at that moment: engine speed, load, coolant temperature, road speed.
This is called freeze frame data.

It is genuinely useful for diagnosis. A fault that only appears at high load
points somewhere different from one that only appears at idle or only when the
engine is cold, and the snapshot is what tells a mechanic which situation to
try to reproduce.

## Why clearing codes is the wrong first move

Clearing a code turns the light off without changing anything mechanical. If
the fault is still present the code returns, usually within a few drives.

Clearing also erases the freeze frame data and resets the readiness monitors,
which are the self-tests the car must complete before an emissions inspection
can be carried out. A car whose codes were just cleared will fail or be
rejected at inspection until it has been driven enough to complete those tests
again.

So codes should be read and recorded before anything is cleared, and clearing
belongs at the end of a repair to confirm the fault does not return, not at the
beginning to make the light go away.

## A code that comes back is telling you something

A fault that was cleared and has reappeared usually means the light was reset
without the underlying cause being repaired. That history is worth more than
the code itself, because it rules out a one-off glitch.

## Codes have limits

Generic fault codes cover the engine, the transmission and the emissions
system well. They cover brakes and tyres poorly: anti-lock braking faults and
tyre pressure faults are usually stored in separate controllers using
manufacturer-specific codes that a general purpose reader often cannot access.

So no fault code appearing does not mean nothing is wearing out. Brake pads and
tyres wear steadily without ever setting a code, which is why wear tracking and
fault codes answer different questions and neither replaces the other.
