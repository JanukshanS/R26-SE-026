## What these codes mean

Petrol gives off vapour, and the fuel vapour system catches it in a charcoal
canister instead of letting it escape. Every so often the engine computer
seals the system and checks that it holds a slight vacuum. If it does not,
it sets a leak code.

P0457 means the leak is at the fuel cap: the cap is loose, missing, or its
seal is not closing. P0455 means a large leak somewhere in the system, and
P0442 a small one. They switch on the engine light, but none of them affects
how the engine runs, and they are not a reason to stop driving.

## What to do first

Check the fuel cap before booking anything. Take it off, look at the rubber
seal for cracks or dirt, and screw it back on until it clicks. Most P0457
codes come from a cap that was not tightened after refuelling.

The light does not go off at once. The computer has to rerun its leak test,
which needs several drives over a few days, so a light that stays on for a
day or two after the cap is tightened is normal.

## How the mechanic finds a leak that is not the cap

If the code comes back with the cap tight, the mechanic runs a smoke test:
harmless smoke is pumped into the vapour system and comes out wherever the
leak is. The usual finds are a perished vapour hose, a cracked filler neck, or
a purge valve that does not close.

## The usual repairs and how long they take

A new fuel cap is the cheapest fix of any fault code and takes a minute to
fit. Replacing a vapour hose or purge valve takes thirty minutes to an hour.
A smoke test itself takes about half an hour.

## What to check afterwards

Ask whether the leak was found with a smoke test or the cap was simply
replaced. If a cap was replaced and the light returns, the leak is somewhere
else in the system and the smoke test should be the next step.
