## What a misfire code means

A misfire means one cylinder failed to burn its fuel properly. The engine
management detects it by watching the crankshaft: each firing cylinder gives
the crank a small push, and a cylinder that does not fire leaves a gap in that
rhythm.

P0300 means the misfire moved around or affected several cylinders. P0301 to
P0308 name one specific cylinder, and the last digit is the cylinder number.
A code naming one cylinder is easier to diagnose, because the fault is almost
always something fitted to that cylinder alone.

## How the mechanic finds the cause

The usual first step costs nothing. The mechanic swaps the ignition coil from
the misfiring cylinder with the coil from a neighbouring one, clears the code,
and drives the car. If the misfire moves to the other cylinder, the coil is
the fault. If it stays where it was, the coil is fine and the problem is the
spark plug, the injector, or the cylinder itself.

The spark plug is inspected next, since it is cheap and quick to reach once
the coil is already out. After that comes a compression or leak-down test on
that cylinder, which checks whether the cylinder can hold pressure at all.

## Why a flashing light means stop

A steady engine light with a misfire means get it looked at soon. A light that
flashes while you drive means the misfire is severe enough to be pushing raw
fuel into the exhaust right now. That is the situation that destroys a
catalytic converter within a short distance, so a flashing light means stop
driving rather than finish the journey.

## What it damages if ignored

Fuel that does not burn in the cylinder burns in the catalytic converter
instead. The converter is not built for that and overheats, and once its
internal structure melts or breaks up it cannot be repaired, only replaced. A
converter costs many times what a coil or a set of plugs costs.

This is the reason a misfire is treated as urgent even when the car still
drives acceptably: the expensive damage happens while the car still feels
fine.

## How long the repair takes

Replacing a single ignition coil is usually under an hour. A full set of spark
plugs is one to two hours on most engines, longer where the inlet manifold has
to come off to reach the rear cylinders. Compression testing adds an hour and
is diagnosis rather than repair, so it is normally charged separately.

## What to check afterwards

Ask which cylinder was at fault and what was actually replaced. Ask whether the
coil swap test was done, because a garage that replaced parts without it may
have guessed. After the repair the code should stay away over several drives;
a code that returns within a week means the real cause was not found.
