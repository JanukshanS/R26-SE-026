## Lean and rich, in plain terms

An engine needs a fixed ratio of air to fuel. P0171 means the mixture is lean:
too much air for the fuel being supplied. P0172 means it is rich: too much
fuel. The engine management detects this from the oxygen sensor in the exhaust
and reports how far it has had to correct, which is the fuel trim reading.

Neither code names a broken part. Both describe a condition with several
possible causes, which is why diagnosis matters more here than parts.

## How the mechanic finds a lean fault

Unmetered air entering after the airflow sensor is the most common cause, so
the intake is checked for leaks first. This is often done by introducing smoke
into the intake and watching where it escapes, which finds cracked hoses and
perished gaskets that are invisible by eye.

If the intake is sealed, attention moves to fuel delivery: fuel pressure at
the rail, a blocked filter, or a tiring pump. The mass airflow sensor is also
checked, since a dirty one under-reports airflow and produces the same
symptom.

## How the mechanic finds a rich fault

The air filter is checked first because it is cheapest. After that come
leaking injectors, a faulty coolant temperature sensor telling the engine it
is still cold, and fuel pressure that is too high. An oxygen sensor that has
aged can also cause it by reporting incorrectly.

## Why P0420 is usually a consequence, not a cause

P0420 says the catalytic converter is no longer cleaning the exhaust well
enough. It is frequently the end result of a fault that was left alone: a
misfire, or a mixture that ran wrong for a long time, damages the converter
over months.

Because of that, replacing the converter without finding what damaged it tends
to produce the same code again on the new part. A garage that quotes for a
converter without first checking fuel trims and misfire history is skipping the
step that decides whether the repair will last.

P0420 can also be caused by a failing oxygen sensor reporting incorrectly,
which is a far cheaper repair, so sensor testing comes before condemning the
converter.

## How long these take

Smoke testing the intake is about an hour. An oxygen sensor replacement is
usually under an hour unless it is seized in place. Catalytic converter
replacement is two to three hours, and it is the most expensive item in this
group by a wide margin.

## What to check afterwards

Ask for the long term fuel trim reading after the repair. Values near zero
mean the mixture is back under control. A repair that leaves the trim still
far from zero has not addressed the cause, even if the light is off, because
clearing the code resets the reading without changing the condition.
