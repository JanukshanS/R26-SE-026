## What an overheating code means

P0217 means the engine computer saw coolant temperature climb past its safe
limit, usually somewhere above 110 to 115 degrees. A healthy engine sits
between about 85 and 100 degrees once warm, and the thermostat and radiator
fan hold it there regardless of traffic or weather.

P0128 is the opposite fault: the engine is taking too long to reach its normal
temperature, almost always because the thermostat is stuck open. It is far
less urgent than P0217 but points at the same part of the car.

Overheating is one of the few faults where continuing to drive turns a
moderate repair into a very expensive one within minutes. Aluminium cylinder
heads warp when they overheat, and a warped head blows its gasket.

## What to do the moment the engine overheats

Pull over safely and switch the engine off. Do not open the radiator cap or
the coolant reservoir while the engine is hot: the system is under pressure
and the coolant is well above boiling, so it can spray out and scald.

Wait at least thirty minutes for the engine to cool, then check the coolant
level in the reservoir. Topping up with clean water gets the car to a garage,
but it does not fix the reason the level dropped. Driving on with the gauge
in the red is the single most expensive mistake a driver can make with this
fault.

## How the mechanic finds the cause

The mechanic starts with the cheap checks. The coolant level and condition
come first: brown, oily or rusty coolant tells a story on its own. Then the
system is pressure tested with a hand pump fitted in place of the radiator
cap, which makes small leaks from hoses, the radiator, or the water pump seal
show themselves as drips.

With the engine running, the mechanic checks that the radiator fan switches on
as the temperature rises, and feels the top and bottom radiator hoses. A hot
top hose with a cold bottom hose means coolant is not flowing through the
radiator, which points at a thermostat stuck closed or a blocked radiator.

If the car has been driven while overheating, a combustion leak test (a
chemical block test) checks the coolant for exhaust gas. Exhaust gas in the
coolant means the head gasket has already failed.

## The usual repairs

Most overheating comes down to one of a few parts. A thermostat stuck closed
is replaced along with its gasket. A leaking or noisy water pump is replaced,
and on many engines it is driven by the timing belt, so both are often done
together to avoid paying the labour twice. A radiator that is blocked inside
or corroded through is replaced rather than repaired. A failed radiator fan
motor or fan relay is replaced.

Whatever the part, the system is refilled with the correct coolant mixed to
the right strength, and then bled of air. Trapped air causes hot spots and
can make a repaired car overheat again on the first drive.

If the head gasket has failed, the cylinder head is removed, checked for flatness
and machined if warped, and refitted with a new gasket. That is engine rebuild
work and costs many times more than the original fault.

## How long the repair takes

A thermostat replacement takes about one to two hours. A water pump takes two to
four hours, more when it sits behind the timing belt. A radiator replacement
takes two to three hours. A head gasket replacement takes one to three days
including machining.

## What to check afterwards

Ask what was found to be causing the overheating, not just which part was
replaced. Ask for the coolant to be pressure tested after the repair. For the
first week, watch the temperature gauge: it should settle in the middle and
stay there in slow traffic. A coolant level that keeps dropping with no
visible leak is a warning sign of a head gasket problem.
