title: How Sri Lankan conditions affect wear

## Stop-start traffic wears brakes by cycles, not distance

Wear on friction parts tracks the number of times they are used far more
closely than the distance travelled. A commute through Colombo traffic can
involve several times as many brake applications per kilometre as the same
distance on the southern expressway. Two cars with identical odometer readings
can therefore have very different amounts of pad left, which is why a
prediction built from actual braking events is more useful than one built from
mileage alone.

## Heat shortens battery life

Sustained high temperatures accelerate the chemical processes inside a lead
acid battery and drive off electrolyte. Batteries in tropical conditions
commonly reach the end of their useful life sooner than the figure quoted by a
manufacturer writing for a temperate market. Under-bonnet temperature in slow
traffic is higher still, because there is little airflow through the engine bay.

## Monsoon rain and tread depth

The wet season turns tread depth from a slow-moving maintenance item into an
immediate safety one. Standing water on main roads is common during heavy
rain, and the margin between a tyre that clears water and one that does not is
smaller than most drivers expect. A tyre that was adequate through the dry
months deserves rechecking before the rains.

## Road surface and suspension

Uneven surfaces, unmarked speed humps and edge breakup put repeated shock
loads through suspension components, wheels and tyre sidewalls. This shows up
as premature bush and damper wear, buckled rims, and alignment that drifts out
sooner than a service schedule assumes. Alignment is worth checking after any
significant pothole impact rather than waiting for the next service.

## Humidity and corrosion

High humidity, and salt air near the coast, corrode battery terminals, brake
disc faces and exposed fasteners. Corroded terminals cause starting faults
that mimic a failing battery. Surface rust on discs after a car has stood for
a few damp days is normal and wipes off in the first few stops, but a car
parked for weeks can develop pitting that does not.

## Short journeys are harder on a car than long ones

An engine that never reaches full operating temperature does not evaporate the
water and fuel that condense into the oil, and the battery never fully
recovers the charge that starting consumed. A pattern of short trips is
mechanically more demanding than the same total distance driven in longer
runs, even though the odometer cannot tell the difference.

## Imported vehicles and parts variation

A large share of vehicles here are imported used, often from markets with
different specifications for the same model name. Two cars badged identically
can take different parts. This is why fitment should be confirmed against the
actual vehicle rather than assumed from the model name, and why a part number
is worth quoting at the counter.
